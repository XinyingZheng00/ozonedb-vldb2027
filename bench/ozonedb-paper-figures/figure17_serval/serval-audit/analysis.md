# Serval-dna issue #1 reproduction: three-way comparison

Fixed-duration (30s per trial, **3 repeats** per backend/N, reported as mean +/- sample standard deviation across repeats) reproduction of serval-dna issue #1's literal scenario -- a real, unmodified `servald` daemon holding its Rhizome store open for the whole trial (a benchmark-only, opt-in `persistent_adder_tick()` hook in `serval-dna/server.c`, gated behind `RHIZOME_PERSISTENT_ADD_INTERVAL_MS`, reusing the same internal functions `rhizome add file` itself calls) racing N independent CLI worker processes running `rhizome add/extract file` against the *same* store, each a fresh subprocess per operation. `sqlite_rest` is the re-architected fix: the daemon is the *only* process that ever opens the store, so instead of an internal adder it gets N+1 REST clients (volume-matched against the other two backends' N workers + 1 daemon = N+1 total add-issuing actors).

Data: `persistent_adder_results/trials.csv`, N in [1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32], 3 repeats each, generated by `run_persistent_adder_benchmark.py`. Figures: `plots/throughput.pdf`, `plots/mean_add_latency.pdf`, `plots/p99_add_latency.pdf` (error bars = SD across repeats; regenerate with `python3 plots/plot_*.py`).

## Findings

**1. The tail latency, not just the mean, is where the real bug shows up -- even at N=1.** With just one CLI worker racing the daemon's own persistent connection, SQLite's p99 add() latency is already 2.0s (SD 0.7s) (mean 63ms (SD 15ms)) -- a real `SQLITE_BUSY`/lock-retry cost, not a modeling artifact. It peaks at 4.6s (SD 0.6s) p99 at N=32. OzoneDB's p99 at N=1 is 20ms (SD 0ms), essentially indistinguishable from its mean, and stays under 117ms at every N tested.

**2. SQLite's throughput doesn't just plateau under contention -- it collapses.** Cycles/sec peaks at 26 (SD 1) around N=8 and then *declines* as N grows further, down to 19 (SD 1) at N=32 -- more concurrent processes make the whole system do strictly less real work per second, not more. Neither OzoneDB (236 (SD 0) at N=32) nor sqlite_rest ever falls below its own low-N throughput this way.

**3. Contention doesn't just slow the CLI side down -- under SQLite it starves the daemon's own steady writes too.** The persistent daemon adder ticks on a fixed 100ms timer, so with no contention it should complete a near-constant number of adds regardless of N. Under SQLite it doesn't: 304 (SD 9) ticks at N=1 down to 142 (SD 21) at N=32 -- the daemon's own connection is itself being starved by the CLI workers' lock contention. Under OzoneDB the daemon's tick count is 301 (SD 0) at N=1 and 303 (SD 1) at N=32 -- essentially unaffected by how many other processes are concurrently writing.

**4. sqlite_rest leads at low N on lighter clients, then OzoneDB overtakes it once its single serialization point starts to bind.** Part of sqlite_rest's early lead is client cost, not architecture: its N+1 clients are plain HTTP calls from an already-running process, not fresh `servald` CLI launches with their own keyring/crypto init, so it's still ahead at N=8 (151 vs OzoneDB's 146 cycles/s). By N=16 the single daemon queue is the bottleneck and OzoneDB has overtaken it (206 vs 155); by N=32 the gap is stark -- sqlite_rest falls to 137 (SD 20) cycles/s (mean wall time for that trial ran 35.4s, SD 4.4s, against a 30s target -- a real backlog, not just a rate drop), while OzoneDB still delivers 236 (SD 0).

**5. Past N=24, sqlite_rest doesn't just slow down -- it drops requests outright.** sqlite/ozonedb had zero failures across every N and every repeat in this sweep. sqlite_rest didn't: at N in [24, 28, 32] some requests hit a real 30s connection timeout (the daemon's request queue never got to them within the whole trial), worst case 8.7 (SD 1.2) failed cycles out of 4783 at N=32 -- a genuine reliability cost of the single-daemon architecture under sustained load, not just the throughput ceiling in finding 4.

**Caveat on sqlite_rest comparisons:** REST clients are structurally lighter than the CLI-launch path used by `sqlite`/`ozonedb` -- each REST cycle is a plain HTTP call from an already-running process, not a fresh `servald` process paying keyring/crypto init every time. Part of its advantage at low N is client cost, not architecture; the fairer read is the throughput-scaling *trend* across the N sweep, not the absolute value at any single low-N point.
